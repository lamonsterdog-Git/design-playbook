Design Principles

The building blocks of every memorable visual experience — from corporate identity to product design.

01
⚖
Balance
Distribute visual weight evenly. Symmetrical balance creates stability; asymmetrical balance creates dynamic tension that guides the eye.
02
◑
Contrast
Juxtapose opposing elements — light/dark, large/small, rough/smooth — to create visual interest and highlight what matters most.
03
⋯
Repetition
Repeat visual elements throughout a design to create consistency and cohesion. Patterns build recognition and brand identity.
04
≡
Alignment
Nothing should be placed arbitrarily. Invisible lines connect elements and create a clean, ordered composition that feels intentional.
05
⬡
Proximity
Related elements should be grouped together. Physical closeness signals relationship — it organises information without explicit labels.
06
□
White Space
Negative space is not empty — it is a design element. It provides breathing room, draws attention to key content, and signals sophistication.
07
▲
Visual Hierarchy
Guide the viewer's eye through size, weight, color, and position. The most important element commands the most visual authority.
08
◉
Color Theory
Color communicates emotion before content is read. Complementary pairs create energy; analogous palettes create harmony; triads create vibrancy.
09
T
Typography
Type is voice made visible. Line-height, tracking, and type pairing determine whether text is readable, skimmable, or poetic.

10
↗
Movement & Flow
Design creates paths for the eye to follow. Lines, arrows, and directional cues guide attention across the composition in a deliberate sequence.
11
⬡
Unity & Variety
Elements should feel like they belong together, yet remain interesting. Too much unity is boring; too much variety is chaotic. Master the tension.
12
⤢
Scale & Proportion
Relative size relationships create meaning. The golden ratio (1:1.618) appears throughout nature and great design — proportion is never accidental.
